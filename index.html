AI2AI 2026 / Project report

Full-duplex assistance for multi-speaker conversations.

Our goal is to build a system that listens and speaks continuously, follows a conversation among multiple people, calls tools, and learns a policy for acting upon or before a user request.

Explore the projectListen to the S2 math experiment

Understand the conversation. Choose when to act.

A useful assistant needs to connect what different speakers say, retain their commitments and constraints, and decide whether an action would help the group. We study the joint problem of what to do and when to do it while the conversation continues.

Conversation

Track multiple speakers

Maintain shared context while keeping each person’s statements, requests, and responsibilities attached to the right speaker. Use earlier discussion to interpret the current utterance.

Action

Coordinate speech and tools

Choose between answering, asking for clarification, and calling a tool. Bring reasoning and tool results back into the conversation while continuing to listen for new information.

Policy learning

React at the right time

Learn when to remain silent, respond to an explicit request, or offer useful assistance before a request. Adapt an ongoing response or pending action when the conversation changes.

The system we are building

The interaction policy should use both the user’s latest words and the wider conversation. An explicit request can trigger a response or tool call. Before a request, earlier discussion may reveal an unresolved need; the policy must decide whether to contribute, ask a focused question, or keep listening.

Full-duplex operation makes these decisions continuous: people can add constraints while the assistant is speaking or waiting for a backend. The same evolving state should govern the spoken response and any pending work.

The DuplexOmni architecture provides the interaction-and-reasoning baseline for the current experiment. Policy learning for multi-speaker conversation and tool calling is the broader project objective.

What the policy should learn

We organize evaluation around three questions: does the system understand whose information matters, does it complete the right response or tool action, and does it enter or yield the conversation at an appropriate moment? Together, these connect content, action, and timing.

The benchmark collection provides complementary protocols for these behaviors. The AMI method supplies history-grounded requests with separate reference facts, so the information available to the assistant stays distinct from the labels used to assess its answer.
