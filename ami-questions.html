AMI / Text-question method

Questions that need earlier meeting history.

We write new, participant-motivated requests for a separate assistant, grounded in the AMI meeting’s earlier words, decisions, and speaker attributions.

How a question is made

  1. 1 / Source

    Read words, speakers, and time

    Use AMI EN2003a’s manual word/segment annotations and speaker IDs. This supplies known speaker attribution, also called oracle diarization. The text input comes from corpus annotations.

  2. 2 / Availability

    Freeze the past at a cutoff

    Group transcript words into chunks closing at sentence punctuation or a 15-second duration cap. A completed chunk becomes available at its end time plus an assumed 0.5 seconds. Keep all available chunks from the meeting start through the cutoff.

  3. 3 / Generation

    Draft a natural request

    Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 and Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking-2507 generate candidates from the available prefix. The Codex research assistant also authors candidates and selects earlier evidence for a supervised 4B condition. Each draft includes a question, proposed answer, atomic claims, and source utterance IDs.

  4. 4 / Context controls

    Check which history matters

    Qwen3-4B Instruct answers the same question with the full prefix, the latest 60 seconds, the question alone, and the prefix with selected evidence removed. Only the question and the chosen transcript condition enter this reader; proposed answers and evidence labels are withheld.

  5. 5 / Review

    Check the words against the meeting

    The Codex research assistant reviews the actual source utterances and reader answers. A retained question should fit the requester’s current task, need an older fact, preserve who committed to what, and distinguish a proposal or upper bound from an agreed plan.

  6. 6 / Output

    Separate requests from labels

    Save question text, requester ID, and source time as the request record. Save proposed answers, required claims, and supporting utterances as separate evaluation labels. Each question is an independent branch from its own source prefix.

What makes a useful question

A question should sound like something a participant could ask an assistant while completing the meeting’s work: recover a commitment, compare an updated plan with an earlier one, or combine a limit with its reason. At least one required fact should precede the most recent minute. The wording should ask for that information without supplying the answer.

Requester perspectives are inferred from prior speech. EN2003a’s metadata identifies channels and speakers but supplies no formal job roles. References are AI-assisted annotations checked against cited speech. The pilot retained 9 variants spanning 7 intents: six assistant-authored questions, two unchanged 30B deadline paraphrases, and one 4B question with an edited reference.

How the controls are read

Review compares the factual content of reader answers across the four history conditions. A full-prefix answer that recovers an older commitment, paired with a recent-only answer that lacks it, supports the question’s history requirement. Removing listed evidence can leave equivalent facts elsewhere, so the remaining transcript is checked too.

The retained set comes from AI-assisted authoring and review. The reader is a local Qwen model and is also used as a candidate generator in one setting. Acceptance records source support and useful wording; reader responses are inspected for correct facts and valid citations.

Models and corpus

The text stages run sequentially on one H100 SXM 80 GB per job, with the active generator or reader on GPU 0. The initial generation, refinement, and supervised-reader stages depend on the preceding outputs. The 4B and 30B models load sequentially; maximum one GPU is active. Transcript preparation, review and publication use 0 GPUs.

Corpus: AMI Meeting Corpus, EN2003a non-scenario meeting, CC BY 4.0. The questions are new synthetic requests; the supporting speech is from AMI. Models: Qwen3-4B Instruct and Qwen3-30B-A3B Thinking.
